Visit Us

Everything here happens on a working farm that is also somebody’s home, so visits are arranged in advance and kept small. The animals set the pace. An animal who walks away is not brought back.


Go on a Trek

Walk a llama on a route through the fields and the treeline, at a pace set by the animal on the other end of the lead rope. You are matched with a member of the trekking team on arrival, based on your height, your pace and your nerve, and that animal is yours for the whole walk. Treks suit older children and adults, and groups are kept to a handful of walkers.

How a trek works:

    Arrive early for the handling lesson. Nobody skips it.
    Meet your llama and learn to read their ears before you take the lead rope.
    Walk the route with a guide, stopping wherever the animals decide to graze.
    Help groom your llama at the end, and take them back to the herd yourself.

Take a Guided Tour

A slower visit for people who would rather watch than walk. A guide takes you through the barns and paddocks, introduces the animals by name, and explains what you are seeing — why that one is standing apart, what that humming means, how an elderly alpaca is fed differently from a young one. Suitable for all ages.


Attend an Event

We run events through the year, and we are building more as we grow. Follow along on social media so you hear about dates as they are set.


Educational Events

Sessions built around one topic, taught by the people who do the work.

    Shearing days, where you can watch the spa team work and handle the fibre as it comes off.
    Camelid care basics, for owners and would-be owners.
    Fibre sessions, following a single fleece from skirting through to spinning.
    Herd health evenings, on caring for animals as they age.

Camelid Enrichments

Enrichment is not decoration. It is how a bored animal stays well. Visitors help build and set out what the herd gets that week, and then stand back and watch a llama work out a puzzle faster than expected.

    Pools in the heat of summer, which the alpacas use and the llamas mostly supervise.
    Pumpkins in the autumn, eaten with enormous seriousness.
    Radios in the barn, because they genuinely do love music.
    Scratching posts, treat boxes and anything new to investigate.

Special Events

Larger gatherings, most of them led by people who came to us with an idea.

    Workshops — felting, spinning, dyeing and craft sessions using fibre from this herd.
    Lecture series — evenings on camelid welfare, behaviour and what sanctuary work actually involves.
    Concerts — music in the field, with the herd at the fence for the sound check.
    Seasonal open days, when the farm is set up for visitors for the whole afternoon.

Yoga

Classes in the pasture in the warmer months, with the herd loose around the mats. The animals are not part of the class and are under no obligation to participate, which they will make clear. All levels welcome.


Join a Program

Programs run over several visits, for people who want more than an afternoon.


Accessible Animal Experiences

Sessions designed with and for people with disabilities, run at the pace the group sets. Where the ground allows, animals are brought to you rather than the other way around.

    Quiet visits with a small group and nothing scheduled.
    Sensory-considered sessions, with reduced noise and a description of the route sent ahead of time.
    Seated grooming, with animals who are comfortable standing alongside a chair.
    Support workers attend free. Tell us about a service animal ahead of time so we can plan the visit around the herd.
